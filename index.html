1 · A hybrid pipeline

Rule-based content selection, LLM realization, and two engagement modules you can switch on independently.

2 · A full game-state corpus

3,276 matches of continuous game state, 142 with professional commentary video. CC0.

3 · A study with real spectators

33 active AoE2 spectators rated engagement and quality of the generated commentary.

Watch a match

Age of Empires II is a real-time strategy game: two players gather resources, advance through four Ages and build armies to raid each other’s economy, on a random map hidden by fog of war. This is the King of the Desert IV grand final, TheViper vs Vinchester, cast by MembTV. This tournament formed the basis for the first dataset in our corpus, which we paired with live video commentary from NOVA.

Video: MembTV on YouTube. SAGE reads the game state behind matches like this one and writes minute-by-minute text updates.

The pipeline

SAGE follows the classic four-stage architecture (Reiter 2007) and runs online, never seeing future events. Watch game events stream past the filters, which take copies, and flow through the four stages into a summary of the recent events. Click a stage to zoom in on what it does. Switch the modules to see which steps they change (coloured borders).

    flagWhat we found
    
  
    A full pipeline from game state to text, tested with its real audience: 33 active AoE2 spectators. The gains are modest but significant, and engagement is still low overall.
    
  
    
    
    Expert Insights helps readers follow the strategy
    With detected strategies, wiki facts and causal explanations, readers enjoyed the competition more (Competitive Nature, β = .20), felt they learned more about playing (Skill Improvement, β = .19) and felt more as if they were playing themselves (Vicarious Sensation, β = .25). The amount of detail also moved closer to “just right” (Detail Level, β = −.24).
    
    
    Event Structuring fixes the order, nothing more
    Grouping parallel events raised only Logicality (β = .22): the updates came in a more sensible order. Readers still missed why events mattered. Our grouping follows simple heuristics; linking events by cause and effect needs real narrative planning.
    
    
    Readable texts, low engagement
    Every engagement motivation except Game Knowledge stayed below the scale midpoint (means 2.5 to 3.9 of 7), while the texts read as ordered and fluent (Logicality 5.6, Readability 4.6). The two modules work independently (no significant interaction), and their effects are small next to how much readers differ from each other.
    
    
    Readers want analysis and context
    Open feedback (96 of 132 ratings) asked for strategic importance and high-level context in a dry, analytical tone, and called the live-update register noise, probably because the updates were read as one text after the match. Trust was low: readers took correct events for hallucinations. One example is Aztec cavalry, normally unobtainable, which the text mentioned without saying how the player got it. Every claim flagged as implausible was a real event in the game data.
    
  
    
  
    Next steps
    Our study used static text read after the match, a format AoE2 viewers do not know; they watch live, video-backed commentary. Two directions follow, and both can be explored with the released corpus: generating commentary alongside video, using the 142 matches paired with professional commentary, and better ways to express causal links between events together with the context needed to understand them. We also evaluated a single proprietary realizer, which limits reproducibility.
